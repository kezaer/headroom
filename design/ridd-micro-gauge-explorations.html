The micro-gauge, explored

Ridd's video review of round 2 landed on one clear winner and one clear instruction: the micro-gauge is the concept, and the next round should sweep every variable around it. This document is that sweep — four axes (form, fill, position, tactility), then the winner at real multi-account scale. Everything else from the video is treated as settled and locked into the frame.

The verdict · 01:57–03:27

Like I actually only need that much space to see where we're at progress-wise. I don't need the full bar."

The proof

At real scale

The micro-gauge's whole pitch is parseability, and that claim is only testable on the dense surface: two providers, multi-account, the settled frame applied. Both popovers use the color-as-event recipe (R2) — left is a degraded day, right is the same surface with everything healthy.

One pushback

The squint question

"I actually only need that much space" is true while reading a mockup at arm's length. But the full-width bar had one real virtue the chip gives up: a length you can read from across the room, without focusing. Before committing, one variant tests whether hover should buy that back.

Detail on demand — the resting state keeps Ridd's clean text rows, the hover state keeps the squint read. If this earns its keep, it's also the natural home for tooltip-grade detail (exact reset time, absolute quota).

·
Or: accept the trade
The numeral is the squint read

The counter-argument: an 18pt semibold numeral is readable from across the room — arguably more so than a bar length. If the numbers carry the distant read and the gauges carry the shape, hover-reveal is machinery without a job. Decide this on the live surface, not in this document: put the chip build on the menu bar for two days and notice whether you ever miss the long bars.

Where this lands

Build the chip, vary only the fill. Form is the least-open question — the chip (or M1, its machined twin) preserved everything Ridd praised, and the exotic forms each give up either alignment, precision, or row height. The genuinely open axis is fill: R2 (color as event) against the reference solid, flipped live like round 2's build test. Keep position as-is — the right-edge reading cluster is most of the concept's speed.

  • Build order: chip + R2 into the real popover behind the demo fixtures, side-by-side with solid-fill. Then the all-healthy day decides "does healthy get a hue" with evidence.
  • Tactility is seasoning, not structure: T1 (proud cap) is the cheapest faithful version of his ask — try it only after the fill question settles, so two variables never move at once.
  • The settled frame (sentence case, chip pill, no email icon, gap-chunked accounts, no dividers) can ship independently of the gauge decision — none of it is blocked on this round.
  • Everything here is dark-first again. The glass fills and lit caps need the same light-mode pass and live glass-card check as the round-2 family before any of it is called done.
  • The severity ramp stays load-bearing (UI-01): whatever recipe wins must keep green→amber→red semantics and propagate to the widget meters through the shared tokens. The micro-gauge is already the widget compact meter's close cousin — this round may quietly unify them.
  • Keep "left" framing (F1) unless a real argument for "used" appears; today every Headroom surface says left, and he didn't object.
